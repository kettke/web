# CHAPTER III

The brilliant light faded into deep darkness. Hanna was disoriented, her heart pounding as she lay on the ground, wondering what had happened. She tried to get up, but failed. After falling heavily, she decided to rest before trying again. Kron lay a few yards away, near what appeared to be the entrance to the small cave where they had found themselves. His breathing was labored, and he seemed no better off than his exhausted companion. The air was stifling, and the temperature was rising. Moisture seeped through the countless cracks in the walls of their cramped shelter, and the silence was broken only by the occasional drip of water falling sharply to the ground. Hanna gradually began to regain her senses and, looking toward Kron with concern, tried to call him.

"Come here!" she said, her voice hoarse and strained.

Her words seemed to encourage her companion, who wagged his slender tail as though trying to answer. But his attempts to reach her were in vain, for both of them were inexplicably exhausted. Hanna then noticed something lying in front of Kron. She could not see it clearly, but he was trying to lick it, growling in frustration when he could not reach it. It looked like some kind of belt, or perhaps a stick or a piece of root. Hanna could not tell what it was, but she remembered that he had been carrying it before the light appeared, so she paid it little attention. There were certainly more important things to think about. Her face reflected the worry the situation had caused her, and her bright eyes seemed to struggle to contain the anguish building inside her with every passing minute. Far from calming down, her restless heart seemed to beat faster and faster.

Although she tried to understand what had happened, no explanation made sense. Gazing drowsily through the cave's narrow opening, she stared in bewilderment at the stars shining brightly in the darkness of the night. Only a few minutes seemed to have passed, yet she had lost all sense of time. The moon seemed to have appeared suddenly, for despite the eclipse, the sun had been shining brilliantly only moments before.

This made her even more confused. She knew her absence would not have gone unnoticed at the school's evening meal. Yet that was no longer her greatest concern. For now, all she wanted was to recover some of her badly diminished strength.

In the end, after hours of anxious wakefulness, both companions succumbed to exhaustion despite their efforts to remain awake, and fell into a deep sleep.

The night passed peacefully. With the arrival of dawn, the first rays of morning sunlight touched Hanna's face, and for a moment she seemed to have forgotten the predicament she was in.

As usual, abandoning the comfort of sleep was no easy task. She resisted waking, waiting for the school's annoying morning bell. But that morning, the bell never rang. Daylight grew stronger, and at last Hanna sat up with a start.

Alarmed, she looked around in astonishment, trying unsuccessfully to work out where she was. A few seconds later, she sprang to her feet, this time without difficulty. The rest had restored some of her strength, and the only lingering physical discomfort seemed to be a slight pain in her neck, probably caused by the awkward position in which she had slept. She paid it little attention. Hanna prepared to leave her unexpected shelter as quickly as possible. She had to get back to school. Thoughts of all the trouble her unintentional disappearance might bring came rushing into her head, and Kron's absence only increased her anxiety. As she was about to leave, however, her foot struck something. It was the belt she had noticed the night before. Hanna bent down and picked it up. It was made of leather, or something similar, and was wrinkled and torn, as though it were very old. But that was not what caught her attention. Attached to it was a small sheath, and inside lay something resembling a dagger.

*What is this?* she wondered, drawing the intriguing object from its sheath.

Hanna examined it with curiosity, for the entire piece was truly beautiful. It was difficult to make out the details beneath the dirt, but it looked more like a jewel than anything else. Its blade was silver-colored and bore a symbol. The hilt was dark and gleaming, with several inlays that looked like precious stones. Hanna was delighted by her discovery. She had never held anything like it before, and without taking her eyes off it, she began wiping it with her shirt sleeve as she made her way outside, momentarily forgetting everything that had happened.

When the sunlight fell upon the dagger, it began to shine in a peculiar way, as though absorbing the sun's energy, growing more beautiful with every passing moment. Hanna was captivated. She continued polishing it while a wonderful sensation flowed through her.

*Who could it belong to?* she wondered, thinking that she had never seen anything like it in her life. It was a piece worthy of a museum.

But when she finally raised her eyes, she was struck speechless. Petrified, she let the dagger fall to the ground. She rubbed her eyes twice, trying to wake herself from what seemed like a magical vision, but in the end she had no choice but to accept the bewildering reality before her.

An immense, rugged cliff stretched out before her. North Park was nowhere to be seen at the foot of the majestic mountain. There were no houses, nor any other sign of civilization. Instead, an endless sea of turquoise water, seemingly motionless, dominated the horizon. The sun shone with extraordinary intensity, and its size was unnaturally large. Every new detail Hanna noticed only strengthened her conviction that she must still be dreaming. She stared in disbelief, struggling to react to what her mesmerized eyes could scarcely comprehend. The air was clean, yet so dense that breathing was almost impossible, and the sky glowed with a strangely brilliant light.

Kron appeared with a loud bark that startled her. She called him over and embraced him, hoping somehow to find a solution to what was clearly a far greater problem than any punishment she had imagined until then. She was alone and lost, but worst of all, she had no idea what strange events had brought her there.

Hoping to find help, she prepared to descend. The way down was steep and dangerous. The mountain rose lifeless around her, with no trace of living things anywhere. No animals, no plants, and certainly no people. There were no paths or signs that anyone had ever traveled through the area. Hanna had not traveled much, but she knew she was very far from home. Without thinking twice, she picked up the dagger again and slipped it into the side pocket of her pants, tightened her boots securely, and set off toward the coast.

At first, she hurried down the slope. She was terribly nervous and wanted to reach the beach as quickly as possible. She had little idea what she would do once she got there, but she did not like this place. The sun shone more fiercely with every passing moment, and the hot air seemed to burn her lungs. At that height, there was nothing but rock upon rock. Some stones were as small as peas, while others were two or three times her size. In fact, the entire mountain looked like nothing more than a heap of badly arranged rocks, as though someone had thrown them down from above and left them wherever they landed. Most appeared to be balanced precariously on top of one another, and under the heavy footsteps of the two companions, they creaked and shifted, making the descent extremely dangerous. Hanna seemed oblivious to the risks of her frantic progress. Even falling hard onto her backside twice on the uneven, painful ground failed to slow her down. But when she climbed onto a large boulder and felt it begin to move, she realized that perhaps remaining in one piece until now had been nothing more than luck.

She sensed the danger immediately. From below, the boulder had looked firmly settled. Kron had crossed it without any trouble. But when Hanna made a great leap onto its surface, she had a feeling something was about to happen. She was right. Before she had time to think, the enormous rock began sliding, slowly at first, emitting a sharp cracking sound as though it were breaking apart. Then, with a sudden violent jolt, Hanna was thrown forward and landed several feet away. The impact was hard, and she rolled several more feet before finally coming to a stop. Dazed and badly bruised, she had struck her head, and her pants were torn at the knee and stained with blood. For a moment, she thought she might lose consciousness. Then an earsplitting noise erupted around her. Only Kron's barking brought her back to her senses. The mountain was coming down!

The rock responsible for the accident had split in two, and one of the halves had been held in place by a small wedge-shaped stone that had finally given way beneath its tremendous weight. Countless rocks began tumbling violently down the slope, while the enormous boulder appeared to be heading straight toward her. At first, Hanna remained motionless, unable to breathe, paralyzed by fear as smaller fragments hurtled past her. The great boulder began gathering speed. Hanna did not react until it was only inches away. She barely moved. Instead, she curled into a ball, convinced that this was the end and there was nothing she could do. But just when she thought the rock would inevitably crush her, it struck another stone directly in front of her and bounced slightly without ceasing to roll. Hanna stayed where she was, squeezing her eyes shut and clenching her teeth as the boulder passed over her, so close that it almost brushed against her. She thought it was all over, and for a second she remained frozen in the same position. Then she turned her head and watched the massive rock continue its unstoppable descent until it finally struck an even larger stone and shattered with a deafening crash, as though it had exploded.

Unable to believe what had just happened, Hanna drew a deep breath. The scorching air rushed painfully into her lungs, but she did not care. In fact, the sensation was almost comforting, for what mattered was that she was still alive. After wiping the sweat from her face with her shirt sleeve and feeling over her body to make sure that, despite the pain, she was still in one piece, she slowly and painfully got to her feet.

"That was close..." she said with a sigh, genuinely believing that only a miracle could have saved her from a tragic end. Never before had she felt death so near.

For a moment she stood motionless, thinking about what had happened, waiting for something, almost as though she expected someone to come and ask whether she was all right. But of course, no one came. She shook the dust from her clothes and took another breath.

*No one's coming to look for me,* she thought miserably.

"Come on, Kron!" she called, summoning her companion so they could continue. Then, overwhelmed by everything that had happened, she burst into uncontrollable tears.

After more than an hour of walking at a brisk pace, exhaustion and hunger began taking their toll on Hanna, and she slowed more and more. Her spirits sank with every step, and an unfamiliar feeling filled her with anguish. Kron, however, trotted along happily, seemingly unaware of what had happened. Somehow, the sight of him gave his companion a little hope, helping her resist the sadness that threatened to overwhelm her in that desolate landscape. Questions crowded her mind like a painful burden, for she could not stop thinking about her circumstances. Could this all be a dream?

Coming upon a stream finally brought them to a halt. Its waters rushed noisily between the rocks. Hanna called to Kron, who already had half his body submerged, and, suspicious of the stream's peculiar appearance, bent down to smell the liquid.

"It's water," she murmured, desperately thirsty.

She drank cautiously until her thirst was somewhat relieved. Then she washed her wounds, which did not look too serious. Kron undoubtedly enjoyed their brief rest much more. Keeping his eyes on his mistress, he made the most of every moment, splashing happily in the water. The break was short but wonderfully refreshing, and thanks to the playful dog, who shook himself with comical enthusiasm in an effort to dry his soaked fur, Hanna managed a faint smile. Even so, her worry continued to grow.

An hour later, the beach was visibly closer, but from where she stood, there was still no sign of anyone who might help. Despite her exhaustion, she quickened her pace. The sight of a few small bushes near the coast encouraged her, for the landscape had been utterly barren throughout their descent. Once at the bottom, beside the shore, she noticed a few plants. But much to her disappointment, there was no trace of anyone who might explain what had happened or tell her what strange place this was. Hanna was very tired and thought it might be a good idea to rest in the shade of a small tree growing nearby. Sitting down facing the surf, she began watching the movement of that intensely colored expanse of water. Its rhythm was hypnotic and strangely slow. Although the sea was almost motionless, the waves broke against the shore with tremendous force. Hanna could not stop marveling at it. Everything around her was filled with details that defied any logic she knew. She still could not believe this was happening, and she clung to the unlikely hope that none of it was real.

After a few minutes, she reached for the dagger. She wanted to examine it again, more carefully this time. But before she could take it out, a loud sound suddenly arose in the distance. At first, it was difficult to tell where it was coming from, but moments later its source became unmistakable. Joy swept over her, for the sound was undoubtedly the rapid galloping of horses. She could not see them, however, because the beach was divided into a series of small coves that severely limited her view.

Just before the riders entered the cove where she was resting, a sound rang out that made her shudder. It was a high-pitched noise that seemed to pierce her head with terrifying force. Frightened, Hanna decided to hide behind the little tree and called to Kron, who was wandering along the shore. But before he could reach her, several riders burst into the cove at incredible speed, immediately attracting the curious dog's attention.

There were four or five horses with riders, and they appeared to be chasing a small animal that was fleeing in terror. Kron stood motionless, watching the unpleasant scene intently. The strange creature seemed badly wounded, and about thirty meters from Hanna's hiding place, it finally succumbed to the pursuit. The riders were extraordinary: enormous, powerful men wearing eccentric armor and mounted on truly bewildering horses, which had thick coats and long black manes. The little animal was equally peculiar. When cornered, it rose into an awkward upright position, although until then it had been running swiftly on all fours. Its cries of panic became words of pleading—or something that sounded very much like them. Hanna could not understand a thing, but the creature's gestures and behavior suggested that it was begging for mercy. One of the riders moved forward and dismounted from his horse, or whatever it was, before approaching the creature. In a powerful, chilling voice, he appeared to say something in a harsh tone. Then, without another word, he kicked the animal—or person, for Hanna could no longer make sense of anything she was seeing. The little creature, as we shall call it, fell violently to the ground and rolled several feet. Immediately, before it could react, the rider grabbed it by its scant, tattered clothing and shook it roughly, causing it to cry out again. Although Kron stood in plain sight, he had gone unnoticed by that group of cruel-looking men, who were so absorbed in what was happening that they seemed to be enjoying the abuse, smiling as they watched.

Seeing the defenseless creature's desperate situation, Kron advanced a few steps and let out a powerful warning bark. The men quickly turned toward the courageous animal, their faces showing great surprise. They stared at him as though they had never seen a dog before. And although they were vastly superior in both size and number, Kron did not retreat an inch. Despite his young age, he bared his formidable teeth.

The man holding the little creature threw it to the ground and hurried toward Kron. Curiosity showed on his face, but his intentions suggested nothing good. Seeing the threatening way the man approached, Kron grew increasingly aggressive and advanced slowly toward him without fear. Suddenly, the man reached for what appeared to be a sword, and the dog decided to attack. With a swift, precise movement, Kron clamped his jaws around the arm the man was using to reach for his weapon, and the force of the attack sent them both tumbling to the ground. Hanna, who had been watching the events unfold in terror, remained trembling in her hiding place, unsure how to react. What could she do? But when she saw the other men advancing toward the struggle, she emerged decisively and shouted for Kron, who was reluctant to release his opponent's arm. Hanna stepped farther forward and called to her friend again. This time, Kron chose to return to her. But it was already too late. Before he had gone more than a few steps, he was captured with the help of a large net.

Hanna then decided to act. She had no idea what to do, but almost without thinking, she rushed toward the men with the reckless hope of rescuing him. Before she could accomplish anything, however, she too was overpowered, but not without putting up a fierce struggle. After hoisting Hanna and Kron onto their horses, the strangers set off toward God only knew what remote destination.
