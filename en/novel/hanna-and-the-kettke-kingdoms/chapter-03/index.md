# CHAPTER III

The brilliant light faded into deep darkness. Hanna lay disoriented on the ground, her heart pounding as she struggled to understand what had happened. Her first attempt to stand ended in failure. She fell heavily and decided to rest for a moment before trying again.

Kron lay a few meters farther ahead, near what appeared to be the entrance to the small grotto in which they had found themselves. His breathing was labored, and he seemed no better off than his exhausted companion.

The air was stifling, and the temperature continued to rise. Moisture seeped through the countless cracks in the walls of their cramped shelter, and the silence was broken only by the occasional sharp patter of falling droplets.

Little by little, Hanna began to regain her senses. Looking toward Kron with concern, she tried to call him.

“Come here!” she said, her voice hoarse and strained.

Her words seemed to encourage him. Wagging his slender tail, he appeared to be trying to answer her. But his attempts to reach her were in vain. Both of them were inexplicably exhausted.

Hanna then noticed something lying in front of Kron. She could not see it clearly, but he was trying to lick it and growling in frustration when he failed to reach it. It looked like some kind of belt, or perhaps a stick or a piece of root. Hanna could not tell what it was, though she remembered that Kron had been carrying it before the light appeared.

She gave it little thought. There were far more pressing things to worry about.

Her face betrayed her growing distress, and her bright eyes seemed to struggle against the anguish building inside her with every passing minute. Far from calming down, her restless heart beat faster and faster.

She tried to make sense of what had happened, but no explanation seemed possible.

Still dazed, she looked through the cave's narrow opening and stared in bewilderment at the stars shining brightly in the night sky.

Only a few minutes seemed to have passed, yet her sense of time had vanished. The moon appeared to have risen out of nowhere. Despite the eclipse, the sun had been shining brilliantly only moments before.

The realization left her even more confused. She knew that her absence would not have gone unnoticed at the school's evening meal. But that was hardly her greatest concern now. Her immediate priority was to recover some of her strength.

At last, after hours of anxious wakefulness, both companions succumbed to exhaustion despite their efforts to remain awake, and fell into a deep sleep.

The night passed peacefully.

With the arrival of dawn, the first rays of morning sunlight fell across Hanna's face. For a little while, she seemed to have forgotten the predicament in which she found herself.

Waking from a pleasant sleep had never been easy for her. As usual, she resisted, waiting for the school's irritating morning bell.

But that morning, no bell rang.

Daylight grew steadily brighter until Hanna suddenly sat up.

Startled, she looked around in astonishment, trying unsuccessfully to work out where she was. A few seconds later, she sprang to her feet, this time without difficulty.

The enforced rest had restored her strength. The only lingering discomfort was a slight pain in her neck, probably caused by the awkward position in which she had slept. It was hardly worth worrying about.

Hanna hurried to leave the shelter. She had to get back to school as quickly as possible.

Thoughts of the trouble her involuntary disappearance might bring came flooding into her mind, and Kron's absence only heightened her anxiety.

As she was about to leave, however, she tripped over something.

It was the belt she had seen the night before.

Hanna bent down and picked it up. It was made of leather, or something very similar, and looked wrinkled and torn, as though it were extremely old.

But its condition was not what caught her attention.

Attached to it was a small sheath, and inside lay something resembling a dagger.

*What is this?* she wondered, drawing the intriguing object from its sheath.

She examined it curiously. The entire piece was extraordinarily beautiful. It was difficult to see clearly beneath the dirt, but it looked more like a jewel than a weapon.

Its blade was silver-colored, with a symbol engraved upon it. The hilt was dark and gleaming, adorned with several inlays that looked like precious stones.

Hanna was delighted by her discovery. She had never held anything like it before.

Still gazing at the dagger, she began cleaning it with the sleeve of her shirt as she made her way outside, momentarily forgetting everything that had happened.

When sunlight fell upon the blade, it began to shine in a peculiar way, almost as though it were absorbing the sun's energy. With every passing moment, it seemed more beautiful.

Absorbed in her discovery, Hanna continued polishing it, a wonderful sensation spreading through her.

*Who could it belong to?* she wondered.

She had never seen anything remotely like it. It was a piece worthy of a museum.

But when she finally raised her eyes, she could scarcely believe what she saw.

Petrified, she let the dagger fall to the ground. She rubbed her eyes twice, desperately trying to wake herself from what seemed like a magical vision.

In the end, she had no choice but to accept the bewildering reality before her.

A vast, rugged mountainside stretched out ahead.

North Park was nowhere to be seen at the foot of that majestic mountain. There were no houses, no buildings, not the slightest trace of civilization.

Instead, an immense sea of turquoise water, seemingly motionless, dominated the horizon.

The sun shone with extraordinary intensity, and its size was abnormally large.

Every new detail only strengthened Hanna's conviction that she must still be dreaming.

She stared ahead, bewildered, struggling to react to what her disbelieving eyes were showing her.

The air was clean, yet so dense that breathing was almost impossible. Above her, the sky glowed with an unfamiliar brilliance.

A loud bark suddenly startled her.

Kron had appeared.

Hanna called him over and threw her arms around him, desperately hoping to find some solution to a problem far greater than any punishment she had imagined receiving at school.

She was alone and lost.

Worst of all, she had no idea what extraordinary events had brought her to this place.

Hoping to find help, she prepared to descend.

The way down was steep and dangerous. The mountain rose around her, lifeless and barren. There were no animals, no plants, and certainly no people.

No paths or other signs suggested that anyone had ever traveled through the area.

Hanna had not traveled much, but she knew she was very far from home.

Without another thought, she retrieved the dagger and slipped it into the side pocket of her pants. Then she tightened her boots securely and set off toward the coast.

At first, she descended in a hurry.

She was terribly nervous and wanted to reach the beach as soon as possible. She had no idea what she would do once she got there, but she wanted to leave the mountain behind.

The sun grew more intense, and the hot air seemed to burn her lungs.

At that altitude, there was nothing but rock upon rock. Some stones were no larger than peas, while others were two or three times her size.

The entire mountainside looked like an enormous heap of carelessly scattered stones, as though someone had thrown them down from above and left them wherever they landed.

Worse still, most appeared to be balanced precariously on top of one another. Beneath the heavy steps of the two companions, they creaked and shifted, making the descent increasingly treacherous.

Hanna seemed oblivious to the danger of her frantic pace.

Even falling hard onto her backside twice on the rough, painful ground did nothing to slow her down.

But when she climbed onto a large boulder and felt it begin to move, she realized that remaining in one piece might have been nothing more than a matter of luck.

She noticed the danger immediately.

From below, the boulder had looked firmly settled. Kron had crossed it without the slightest difficulty.

But the moment Hanna made a great leap onto its surface, she sensed that something was wrong.

And she was right.

Before she could react, the massive stone began sliding slowly, producing a sharp, grinding creak, as though it were splitting apart.

Then, with a sudden violent jolt, Hanna was thrown forward.

She landed a couple of meters away, the impact sending her tumbling farther down the slope before she finally came to a stop.

Dazed and badly bruised, she struggled to understand what had happened.

She had struck her head, and the fabric of her pants was torn at the knee and stained with blood.

For a moment, she thought she was about to lose consciousness.

Then a deafening noise erupted around her.

Only Kron's frantic barking brought her back to her senses.

The mountainside was collapsing!

The boulder that had caused the accident had split in two. One half had remained precariously supported by a small wedge-shaped stone, but the tremendous weight had finally forced it loose.

Countless rocks began crashing down the slope, while the enormous boulder appeared to be heading straight toward her.

At first, Hanna could only stare.

Breathless and paralyzed by fear, she watched the smaller fragments hurtle past her.

Then the great boulder began gathering speed.

She did not react until it was only inches away.

Even then, she barely moved. She simply curled up, convinced that the end had come and that there was nothing she could do.

But just as the boulder seemed certain to crush her, it struck another rock directly in front of her and bounced slightly upward without stopping its descent.

Hanna remained curled up, her eyes squeezed shut and her teeth clenched as the massive stone passed over her, so close that it almost brushed against her body.

For a moment, she thought everything was over.

She stayed motionless for another second before turning her head.

The boulder was still racing down the mountainside, unstoppable, until it finally struck an even larger rock and shattered with a deafening crash, as though it had exploded.

Unable to believe what had happened, Hanna drew a deep breath.

The scorching air burned its way into her lungs, but she did not care. In fact, the sensation was almost comforting.

She was alive.

Wiping the sweat from her forehead with her shirt sleeve, she carefully felt her body, making sure that despite the pain, everything was still intact.

Then she slowly and painfully got to her feet.

“That was close…” she whispered with a sigh.

She was certain that only a miracle had saved her from a terrible death. Never before had she felt death so near.

For a moment, she stood still, thinking about what had happened, almost waiting for someone to appear and ask whether she was all right.

But, of course, nobody came.

She shook the dust from her clothes and took another deep breath.

*No one's coming to find me,* she thought miserably.

“Come on, Kron!” she called, summoning her companion so they could continue.

And then, overwhelmed by everything that had happened, she burst into uncontrollable tears.

After more than an hour of walking at a brisk pace, hunger and exhaustion began to take their toll.

Hanna slowed with every step. Her spirits sank, and an unfamiliar feeling of anguish weighed heavily upon her.

Kron, however, trotted along cheerfully, seemingly unaware of the danger they had escaped.

Somehow, the sight of him gave Hanna hope. She refused to surrender completely to the sadness of that desolate landscape.

Questions crowded her mind, each one a painful burden she could not set aside.

Could this all be a dream?

Coming upon a stream finally brought them to a halt.

Water rushed noisily between the rocks.

Hanna called to Kron, who was already half submerged. Suspicious of the stream's peculiar appearance, she bent down and sniffed the liquid.

“It's water,” she murmured, desperately thirsty.

She drank cautiously until her thirst was at least partly satisfied.

Then she washed her wounds, which fortunately did not look too serious.

Kron undoubtedly enjoyed the brief rest far more than she did. Keeping an eye on his mistress, he spent every possible moment splashing happily in the stream.

The stop was short but wonderfully refreshing.

And when the playful animal shook himself vigorously, sending droplets flying from his soaked coat, Hanna managed a small smile.

Even so, her anxiety continued to grow.

An hour later, the beach was finally coming into view.

But from where she stood, there was still no sign of anyone who might help her.

Despite her exhaustion, Hanna quickened her pace.

The sight of a few small bushes near the shore encouraged her. Since beginning the descent, she had seen nothing but a barren, lifeless landscape.

At the foot of the mountain, beside the coast, she found a handful of plants.

But to her disappointment, there was still nobody who could explain what had happened or tell her where she was.

She was terribly tired and decided that resting in the shade of a small tree might be a good idea.

Sitting down near the surf, she watched the vast expanse of water rise and fall.

Its rhythm was hypnotic and strangely slow.

Although the sea appeared almost motionless, the waves struck the shore with tremendous force.

Hanna could only stare in amazement.

Everything around her was filled with details that defied any logic she knew.

She still could not quite believe what was happening. Somewhere deep inside, she clung to the faint, improbable hope that none of it was real.

After a few minutes, she reached for the dagger.

She wanted to examine it again, more carefully this time.

But before she could take it out, a loud noise began to rise in the distance.

At first, she could not tell where it was coming from.

A few moments later, however, the sound became unmistakable.

Her heart leaped with joy.

It was unmistakably the sound of galloping mounts!

She could not see them yet. The coastline was broken into a series of small coves that severely limited her view.

Just before the riders entered the cove where she was resting, a piercing sound rang out.

It was so sharp and terrifying that it seemed to bore straight into her head.

Frightened, Hanna decided to hide behind the little tree.

She called to Kron, who was wandering along the shore, but before he could reach her, several riders burst into the cove at an astonishing speed.

The sudden commotion immediately caught the curious dog's attention.

There were four or five horses with riders, and they appeared to be pursuing a small animal fleeing in terror.

Kron stood motionless, watching the disturbing scene.

The strange creature seemed badly wounded, and about thirty meters from Hanna's hiding place, it finally succumbed to the pursuit.

The riders themselves were extraordinary.

They were enormous, powerfully built men wearing bizarre suits of armor. Their mounts were equally bewildering: horses, if that was what they were, with thick coats and long black manes.

The little creature was peculiar too.

When it found itself cornered, it rose awkwardly onto its hind legs. Until that moment, it had been running swiftly on all fours.

Its terrified cries suddenly became pleading words—or something resembling words, at least, for Hanna could not understand a thing.

Still, its gestures and desperate manner left little doubt that it was begging for mercy.

One of the riders moved forward and dismounted from his horse—or whatever the animal might have been.

He approached the creature and appeared to address it in a powerful, chilling voice, his tone harsh and threatening.

Then, without another word, he kicked it.

Hanna no longer knew whether to think of the creature as an animal or a person. Nothing she was witnessing seemed possible.

The small being—let us call it that—fell violently to the ground and rolled several feet.

Before it could recover, the rider seized it by its scant, ragged clothing and shook it roughly, provoking another terrified scream.

Although Kron stood in plain sight, the group of cruel men had not noticed him.

They were too absorbed in their abuse of the helpless creature, smiling as though they were enjoying themselves.

Seeing the small being's desperate situation, Kron advanced a few steps and let out a powerful, defiant bark of warning.

The men turned toward the courageous dog.

Their faces registered genuine astonishment.

They stared at him as though they had never seen a dog before.

And although they vastly outnumbered and outweighed him, Kron did not retreat.

Despite his young age, he bared an impressive set of teeth.

The man holding the small creature threw it to the ground and strode toward Kron.

Curiosity showed on his face, but his intentions appeared anything but friendly.

Sensing the threat, Kron grew more aggressive.

Fearlessly, he advanced toward the man, one slow step at a time.

Suddenly, the rider reached for what appeared to be a sword.

Kron attacked.

With astonishing speed and precision, he sank his teeth into the arm the man was using to reach for his weapon.

The force of the attack sent them both crashing to the ground.

Hanna had watched the confrontation unfold in terror.

She remained hidden, trembling, unable to decide what to do.

What could she possibly do?

But when she saw the other riders advancing toward the struggle, she emerged from her hiding place and shouted for Kron.

The dog refused to release his opponent's arm.

Hanna stepped forward and called again.

This time, Kron obeyed and turned back toward her.

But it was already too late.

Before he had covered more than a few yards, the men captured him with a large net.

Hanna then decided to act.

She had no idea what to do, but almost without thinking, she rushed toward the men with the reckless hope of rescuing him.

Before she could accomplish anything, however, she too was overpowered—but not without putting up a fierce struggle.

After hoisting Hanna and Kron onto their horses, the strangers set off toward God only knew what remote destination.
