# IN A PLACE, IN A TIME…

Gunuk stepped onto solid ground, happy to have completed the long journey by boat. Still a little dizzy, he said goodbye to the kindly fisherman.

“Thank you. Without your help, I would not have made it,” he said, his gaze sincere. “Remember! A weighty secret you now carry. Guard it, you must. Seek only Vendemur.”

From the boat, Bauros looked back on their conversation during the crossing, his expression grave. Gunuk had spoken sparingly, but his words had been revealing enough for Bauros to understand the importance of the information now entrusted to him.

“But where? He's not easy to find. He rarely lets himself be seen,” Bauros asked.

“In Antraka, you must await his arrival,” Gunuk replied, looking toward the great mountain rising before him, eager to begin the climb.

“I'll help you! You're exhausted, wounded… If you told me where you're going, couldn't I make the journey easier for you? My body is beginning to feel its age, but I still have the strength of my youth. I've always been loyal to Kettke! You have nothing to fear,” Bauros insisted, reluctant to hoist the sails and leave.

“To Antraka, your path must lead. Mine… may have no return.”

“I'm sure you'll come back.”

“That matters little now,” Gunuk answered gravely, convinced of what he had just said.

The fisherman fell silent for a moment. Gunuk was clearly not joking. His courage was worthy of the greatest of the Kinorians. At last, Bauros unfurled the sails and slowly made his way out to sea.

“Good luck! It was an honor to meet you. You're a remarkable being! Have no doubt that one day your story will be told. In a world at peace!” Bauros called out as Gunuk, too, left the beach behind and began climbing the rocky terrain.

The Island of Rocks was an inhospitable place, barren and uninhabited. It always had been, and that day would be no exception. In a way, Gunuk was glad he would not have to hide from prying eyes. Yet the steep, rugged path would be anything but easy. The slight unsteadiness of his steps only deepened his exhaustion, and what he now carried was a heavy burden. Not merely in the physical sense. It was far more complicated than that.

He had been chosen. Tuarkket had entrusted it to him on his deathbed—his king, his friend, the one who had risen again from darkness to sow the seeds of hope once more. Could Gunuk ever have imagined a greater honor?

He could not fail. There was no room for error in this mission.

The memory of what had happened sent a shiver through him from head to toe. Dark thoughts, every one of them. Kettke, the Kingdom of Kingdoms, had fallen prey to treachery and evil. Who could have imagined, only days earlier, that such a thing would happen?

Overwhelming anguish seized Gunuk. Only he could change what had happened. Only he carried the flame of hope. Only he…

After hours of walking, utterly exhausted, he stopped beside a great boulder that cast a welcome patch of shade. He drew aside his tunic and gazed at his precious cargo.

It seemed to give off a heavenly light, like the first rays of dawn.

So small, and yet so great!

Gunuk smiled as he thought of his family, of his son.

“He will finish the journey you must begin today,” Vendemur, the Narako, had told him before he left.

The thought that his life might soon come to an end did not frighten him in the least. He had always believed life was a fleeting journey. What terrified him was the possibility of dying before he reached his destination. That was what must give meaning to his life—and perhaps to his death.

What good would it be to go on living in a world ruled by tyranny?

He shook his small head, trying to drive away the multitude of thoughts that threatened to cloud his composure, and set off once more.

The sun blazed overhead, its fierce rays seeming to sear his back. Sweat had begun to break out all over his body. He knew he had to hurry. Darkness would soon fall.

Glancing up at the radiant sun, he noticed with concern how close the moon had drawn.

There was no time to lose!

Kudark would be eager to emerge from his lair, impatient to travel—even if only in spirit—to behold his long-awaited spoils.

Gunuk smiled again.

“A bitter disappointment he will find,” he whispered to himself.

But then he considered how much greater Kudark's fury would be, and that was no good thing.

Gunuk quickened his pace. He was hungry, so exhausted he could barely lift his legs, and his wounds made every movement increasingly painful. But what did any of that matter now?

As though the moon had begun to devour the sun, daylight slowly yielded to the inevitable phenomenon. An unsettling twilight spread across every corner of the island, and Gunuk, with an effort that seemed to consume him from within, finally located the grotto.

There was no time to celebrate. Every second mattered.

By the time darkness had nearly reached its deepest point, Gunuk staggered into the small cave.

What awaited him beyond that doorway into unknown worlds? What was he supposed to do once he crossed it? Vendemur had described the route that would bring him here, but… what came afterward?

Despite the agonizing questions, he drew a deep breath, comforted by the knowledge that he had finally reached the place. Yet he was more certain than ever that he would never return.

He turned his head, hoping for one last glimpse of the world where he had been born and raised.

But before he could look upon his homeland for the final time, the moon and sun merged into one, and an overwhelming light erupted from the depths, engulfing him with savage force.
