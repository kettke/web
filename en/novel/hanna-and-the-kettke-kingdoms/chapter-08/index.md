# CHAPTER VIII

For Hanna, the night had been exhausting. Although she had fallen asleep easily enough, she had spent most of it drifting between nightmares and the constant awakenings caused by her aching leg. When she finally slipped into a peaceful sleep, not even the rays of the midmorning sun could wake her.

Gunuruk, meanwhile, was already up. Snorks were peculiar creatures even when it came to sleeping. Sometimes they needed only a few hours to regain their strength, but when they were truly exhausted, they could fall asleep in the most unlikely places.

The loud ringing of the lunch bell finally left Hanna no choice but to wake up. Gunuruk hurried down the stairs and enthusiastically roused his young companion, who waved her arms in protest, unwilling to abandon her comfortable position.

“Let, let, Hanna!” he repeated insistently.

A few minutes later, the two were aboard the cart, heading toward the inlet.

Once Hanna was fully awake, her yawns gave way to the rumbling of her stomach. She cheerfully made her hunger known to Gunuruk, who, with his characteristic smile, encouraged her to eat her fill. She had a demanding day ahead of her.

The two companions arrived at the dining hall a little late. By the time they entered, most of the diners were already caught up in their usual frenzy.

Hanna wasted no time. Taking her seat beside Yasnek once again, she began devouring the assortment of food spread across the table.

When everyone had finished eating, the strange drink was served, as usual. Curious, Hanna asked Gunuruk what it was called.

“Dudok!” Yasnek answered before the young Snork could speak. Gunuruk's mouth was so full that he could not have managed a word anyway.

Then came the customary period of conversation, accompanied by so much noise that it was not unusual for one of the participants to fall asleep right on the table.

Hanna found this enormously amusing.

She also tried to speak with the Snorks around her, who seemed eager to include her in their conversations, gesturing in all sorts of unusual ways. But although she was beginning to recognize the language, understanding what they said was still beyond her.

Time passed quickly, and the bell calling everyone back to work rang out powerfully, its sound reverberating through the dining hall.

Gunuruk was completely absorbed in a peaceful sleep, while Hanna had taken the opportunity to explore the building, whose interior was decorated with beautiful wood carvings.

Her presence was becoming increasingly familiar, and few Snorks seemed surprised to see her anymore.

Noticing that most of them were already leaving, she returned to their table.

Yasnek had to strike it sharply with his hand. The bell had failed to wake young Gunuruk, who sprang upright with a tremendous start.

Once he had recovered from the fright, the three joined the crowd making its way outside.

There, the Uru said goodbye to them.

“Little Snork, the human looks well today. Her bright eyes tell me she wishes to understand us too. Forget not that, as the Great Aknus instructed, your duty is to show her Bettnuk,” Yasnek said.

“So I shall, great Uru,” Gunuruk replied.

After fetching their Lewik, the two set off toward the hill.

As Yasnek had observed, Hanna seemed to be feeling much better. Her eyes betrayed her growing enthusiasm, and although her recovery was far from complete, she was undoubtedly in good spirits.

They were already making their way toward the hill.

Hanna admired the scenery in amazement. It was the first time she had visited this part of the great village.

Gunuruk, meanwhile, was sweating profusely beneath the sun, which still showed little inclination to surrender its strength. He concentrated on guiding the cart.

The eastern part of Antraka housed most of the population, yet it was surprisingly inconspicuous. The Snorks lived in small burrows excavated into the hard ground of the Kett-Onk hillside, near the inlet at its northeastern end.

From there, it was possible to see a spectacular waterfall formed by a broad, tranquil river flowing down from the high mountains of Ultur. At the edge of the land, its waters plunged violently into the sea, merging with the salty ocean below.

Their destination was not far away. On foot, they would have reached it in only a few minutes.

The little cart, however, required a longer route, and the journey took considerably more time than expected.

Before long, Hanna began to feel every bump along the uneven track.

Following the winding road, they eventually reached their destination. As the sun sank toward the horizon, they were rewarded with magnificent views of the approaching sunset.

Urduk, the great master, was waiting impatiently.

He breathed a sigh of relief when he saw them arrive.

Little Snorkys scampered noisily in every direction, while adult Snorks crowded the area, hurrying back and forth without pause.

“Come, young ones! Your arrival I have been awaiting!” he exclaimed, impatiently beckoning them with his arm.

Urduk was old—very old—and was responsible for the sawmills at the top of the hill.

There, enormous tree trunks were received, stripped of their bark, cut, and sent through an extensive network of canals to the various hangars beside the inlet.

Next to the sawmills stood immense weaving workshops. After the woody fibers of the great trees growing in the nearby forests had been treated, they were transformed into Antraka's valuable fabrics, prized throughout the Known Land for their many remarkable qualities.

At this point, the river was held back before it could plunge over the precipice, and its power was channeled to assist the Snorks in their work.

Although their heavy, stocky bodies made swimming impossible, their lives had been closely tied to water ever since they had abandoned the great mining mountains of Sudum in times beyond memory.

Hanna and Gunuruk entered the sawmill with the old master.

Unlike the buildings Hanna had seen before, this sturdy structure was remarkably simple. It was spacious, with smooth, solid walls and extraordinarily high ceilings.

It was so large that a narrow canal flowed directly through its center, carrying logs and irregular pieces of timber.

The wood accumulated against small barriers, where the Snorks could easily reach it.

Hundreds of them worked inside, engaged in a frenzy of activity that seemed to follow no discernible order. Everywhere Hanna looked, creatures were performing an astonishing variety of tasks.

She walked forward in amazement, thoroughly enjoying the visit.

Inside the sawmill, she was able to observe the entire process. Given the enormous size of the logs, the spectacle was extraordinary.

The timber was selected, stripped, cut, and prepared for transport to its destination.

As Urduk and Gunuruk chatted, the three continued what had become a guided tour.

Hanna was fascinated. In excellent spirits, she watched everything around her, determined not to miss a single detail.

Despite the apparent chaos, she gradually realized that the work was meticulously organized.

Every Snork and Snorky was completely focused on a particular task.

Clearly, these creatures developed their remarkable capacity for work at a very young age.

The pieces of wood traveling along the canals were called *puks*, regardless of their size or type.

The Snorks handled them with extraordinary efficiency.

With astonishing skill, a single Snork could lift a large puk from one of the canals running throughout Antraka.

It was an impressive achievement, considering the tremendous weight and size of the logs.

It was also dangerous.

A single mistake could place a worker's life in serious jeopardy. The same water that made so much of their work possible remained a constant threat to their safety.

Hanna was speechless.

When they reached the far end of the building, they stopped.

Through enormous windows, she discovered a breathtaking view.

Darkness was steadily claiming that corner of the land, but a great moon shone with unusual brilliance, illuminating much of Antraka.

From the summit of Kett-Onk, Hanna could see the great dining hall, the hangars, the docks crowded with enormous ships, the beautiful inlet, and almost the entire northern section of the village.

At that hour, the area was teeming with Snorks.

“Let! Let!” Gunuruk called.

His companion seemed completely absorbed in the idyllic view of the place that, for the time being, had become her home.

They left the building through the opposite end and continued admiring the scenery.

From there, Hanna could see the intricate network of canals running throughout Bettnuk. Beginning at the nearby waterfall, they descended toward the coast, carrying countless puks that accumulated along the shoreline.

Next, they visited the weaving workshops.

The building resembled the sawmill, although it was considerably smaller.

Most of the work there was performed by female Snorks, who carried out their tasks with a skill and ease that could easily make the process appear simpler than it really was.

Hanna noticed something unusual as soon as she entered.

Many Snorky infants, still too young to help, were sleeping throughout the workshop.

Others, hungry and restless, seemed to complicate their mothers' work by clinging to them and nursing.

It was the first time Hanna had witnessed such a scene, and she was fascinated.

Yet the mothers behaved so naturally that she quickly understood this was perfectly ordinary.

Snorkys remained dependent on their mothers for a considerable time. In fact, it was rare to see an adult female without one or two noisy little ones following her throughout the day.

This visit was shorter.

After a brief look around the facilities, they returned outside.

The darkness made the countless bonfires, lamps, and torches scattered throughout the village shine all the more brightly.

For a few moments, a cold breeze brushed against the young companions' faces.

Then Gunuruk led the way toward their next destination.

Only a short distance from the edge of the hill, a steep staircase carved into solid rock descended toward an ornate doorway.

Beyond it lay a dwelling that seemed to extend deep into the heart of the earth.

It was the home of Ynack, the most famous Snork on the island.

Known as *the Traveler*, she had certainly not led the peaceful, routine existence typical of her people.

Quite the opposite.

Long ago, the elderly Snork had participated in so many journeys and important events that she had acquired a depth of knowledge highly unusual among her kind.

Indeed, the Council had sought her wisdom on numerous occasions.

Those exceptional abilities now served her community.

For many years, ever since her declining physical strength had prevented her from continuing to explore the world, Ynack had devoted herself to classifying the objects collected during her travels, recording her eventful adventures, and, above all, helping any Snork who came to her to understand the difficult art of signs and words.

Despite the satisfaction such knowledge might offer, her home was hardly a crowded place.

Snorks were not especially inclined toward intellectual pursuits.

Nevertheless, some of them, driven by a growing desire to find answers beyond the small peninsula of Antraka, had managed to achieve the difficult distinction of becoming guides.

It was a highly respected title.

A guide was recognized for an advanced command of language, the ability to interpret written documents, and many other qualities whose true significance only a Snork could fully appreciate.

Ynack introduced herself with great courtesy.

Hanna immediately sensed that the elderly creature possessed an extraordinary wealth of experience.

Wrinkles covered her face, and the whitish fur around her small snout and eyelids revealed her considerable age.

Her calm gestures and warm manner made Hanna feel welcome from the very first moment.

A small fire and several oil lamps illuminated the dwelling.

Documents were piled on uneven shelves. Books and loose sheets of paper gave the room a somewhat disorderly appearance, although everything seemed to have its proper place.

Enormous windows fitted with thick, translucent glass looked out from the dwelling.

Hanna could not resist leaning toward a small opening through which a pleasant breeze was blowing.

After admiring the beautiful views from this unusual location, she followed Gunuruk through the spacious house.

Although its ceilings were rather low, the dwelling extended over a considerable area.

Ynack's entire home could have been considered a magnificent museum.

Among its treasures were Tuark teeth, claws from the rare Nuruks, carved figurines representing the inhabitants of Argon, and countless drawings, maps, and other illustrated documents that would have been impossible to find anywhere else in Antraka.

Hanna explored everything with tremendous curiosity, touching and smelling whatever she encountered.

Then she noticed a series of illustrated panels depicting various scenes.

Something about them compelled her to stop and examine the intricate images more closely.

She did not know it, but she was standing before one of the oldest and most valuable works of art created by the Mibuks.

The illustrations depicted the battle that had marked the beginning of the modern era: the overthrow of the Great King Oddeon.

It was the battle that had ended the last reign in Kinoria, brought down the magnificent palace of Kettke, and ushered in a new age dominated by constant warfare and instability throughout the sovereign kingdom.

All of this was explained beneath the individual panels.

Hanna, however, could not read the inscriptions. The strange written language was still entirely unfamiliar to her.

What captivated her was the terrifying power of the images themselves.

Their realism and vividness were so extraordinary that the figures almost seemed capable of moving and speaking.

Some scenes appeared to draw her into the savage battle, surrounding her with its terror.

Hanna experienced sensations unlike anything she had ever felt before.

As usual, Gunuruk eventually had to give her a gentle tug to persuade her to continue.

There were so many other strange things to see that she soon forgot the unsettling feeling.

At last, they returned to the entrance.

Gunuruk pointed toward an enormous tapestry that appeared to represent a map.

The elaborate weaving technique made it possible to distinguish the places depicted in remarkable detail.

“Nit buk Hanna, Antraka et!” her plump companion announced.

Hanna guessed that she was finally being shown the place where she had spent all this time.

Unfortunately, the image did little to clarify matters.

The peninsula represented on the tapestry bore no resemblance to anything she had studied in geography, although this hardly surprised her.

Moreover, every name was written in the strange local language.

Even after examining the tapestry carefully, she could learn little more.

Following their brief tour, the three sat together for a while and attempted a conversation.

Ynack understood Hanna's limitations and, with exceptional teaching skills, managed to draw her into the discussion.

It was a very simple exchange.

Nevertheless, it awakened in Hanna an overwhelming desire to learn everything she could about this mysterious land that had welcomed her with such hospitality.

After those absorbing moments, the two friends decided to return home.

They climbed slowly into the cart. Hanna's leg had endured a demanding day.

The journey back offered yet another new experience.

Taking the road westward, which was wider and had a more even surface, they entered the dense forest.

There were many people among the trees, and at their slow pace, the cart seemed to be in everyone's way, no matter where they tried to go.

Despite the bustle, Hanna could not help thinking how frightening that part of the forest would have been if they had been alone.

Even with the numerous lamps carried by those around them, the immense canopy of the towering trees barely allowed the great moon's light to penetrate.

Before long, the glow of the village began to appear once again above the highest branches.

They finally reached Bettnuk's southern entrance.

Hanna breathed a sigh of relief as they left the forest behind.

A strong breeze was now moving through the trees, making their branches sway and creak ominously.

She glanced back.

One final shiver ran through her body.

Bettnuk was even more crowded than before.

To her astonishment, Hanna spotted several people who looked like her.

Humans.

Her heart began to race.

Yet much as she wanted to attract their attention, she resisted.

Following Gunuruk's instructions, she covered herself with a large, loose cloak.

From that moment onward, it would become her anonymous disguise.

The arrival of the southern winds brought visitors of every race, kind, and background to Antraka, especially Bettnuk.

Not all of them were honorable.

Some possessed thoroughly questionable reputations, and it was wise to remain cautious.

Hanna already knew from bitter experience how dangerous it could be to fall into the hands of the wrong people.

The two friends eventually reached their cabin.

After lighting a small fire, which made the room considerably more comfortable, they soon fell into a deep sleep.

Gunuruk rested for a while, then recovered his strength and left to help his busy companions.

Hanna, meanwhile, slept peacefully, barely changing position throughout the night.

The following morning dawned bright and clear.

Hanna woke with a vitality she had not experienced in a long time.

As usual, the first rays of sunlight roused her.

Gunuruk had not yet returned.

Hungry, she decided to prepare breakfast herself.

It seemed a simple enough task. After all, she had watched her companion do it day after day.

But as time passed, she discovered that rekindling the extinguished fire was far more difficult than expected.

The Snorks used a peculiar kind of polished stone for the task, and Hanna could not make the stones work.

No matter what she tried, she achieved nothing.

Fortunately, the little Snork returned before long.

Finding his companion still struggling determinedly with the fire, unwilling to surrender, he let out an enormous and thoroughly peculiar laugh.

After enjoying a substantial breakfast, the two set off once again for Ynack's house.

Hanna did not know exactly where they were going, but she suspected they would return to the fascinating dwelling.

The thought filled her with excitement.

Throughout the journey, she reflected on how carefully she ought to examine everything there.

Sometimes people failed to appreciate what stood directly before them.

Only after it was gone did they realize how little they had enjoyed it.

When they reached their destination, time seemed to fly.

Hanna followed Ynack's instructions with complete attention.

Her passion for learning drew her ever deeper into the mysteries of that unknown and intriguing world.

She wanted to know everything.

Indeed, neither that day nor on the days that followed did she return to the dining hall for lunch with the other Snorks.

Instead, she enjoyed Ynack's hospitality.

The elderly Snork proved to be an excellent cook, and Hanna sampled many traditional dishes from the local cuisine.

Some, it must be admitted, were not particularly appealing to a human palate.

Little by little, the home of that endearing Snork began to feel like Hanna's own.

Despite the constant stream of visitors, Ynack made every effort to devote time to her remarkable student.

Day after day, Hanna seemed to make enormous progress in the difficult task she had undertaken.

Her relationship with Gunuruk changed as well.

The young Snork was needed for the many jobs that inevitably accumulated during this important season.

Nevertheless, each morning and evening, he remained responsible for taking Hanna to and from the cabin that continued to serve as her sleeping quarters for several more days.

He spent every free moment with her, enjoying each instant they shared.

Always cheerful, he took tremendous satisfaction in her steady progress.

As her understanding improved, communication between them became much easier.

More than once, Hanna's curiosity placed him in an awkward position.

Although he concealed his discomfort rather well, the little Snork was obliged to protect secrets that, for the time being, he was duty-bound to keep.

Sometimes this was extraordinarily difficult.

His inquisitive friend had become someone very special to him.

A few weeks later, Hanna seemed capable of speaking with reasonable fluency.

Her vocabulary was still limited, and her choice of words was often inaccurate, but she took enormous pride in every achievement.

She delighted in satisfying this most basic need to communicate.

With the same enthusiasm she had shown on her first day, she seized every opportunity to ask questions of anyone who might help her—or at least understand her, since she did not always manage to say what she intended.

As Hanna's abilities developed, she began noticing that certain objects she had originally seen in Ynack's house were no longer there.

Others had been disguised or concealed in an attempt to make them less noticeable.

Most of the time, these efforts were unsuccessful.

Gunuruk also seemed increasingly reluctant to answer particular questions.

Sometimes he became visibly nervous when Hanna raised certain subjects.

Ynack avoided those same subjects with remarkable skill.

Hanna began to suspect that they were hiding something from her.

In truth, she learned relatively little about the wider world and never discovered how she had arrived there.

Eventually, however, the days passed and the question ceased to trouble her quite so much.

She felt increasingly at home in that strange land, although memories of her recent past still brought occasional tears.

Her injured leg did not recover as quickly as her knowledge of the language improved.

Nevertheless, with time, it gradually returned to its former excellent condition.

At first, Gunuruk was somewhat concerned.

The Council had declared that Hanna would retain her special status only while she was recovering.

Once she was well again, she would have to leave.

Aknus, however, intervened.

With the help of Mettmek, who was responsible for keeping the Council informed of Hanna's progress, he ensured that even when she was in perfect health, the healer continued to advise against a long journey.

When Hanna was finally able to move without difficulty, she was transferred to one of the small burrows on the hillside.

It was a safer location, farther from the bustle of the central village.

Gunuruk remained at her side, as always.

Initially, he reacted with some irritation to yet another move.

But after his grandfather explained the reasons, he accepted that it was the best way to preserve Hanna's anonymity.

Every day, the village filled with an increasingly varied assortment of visitors.

Hanna, careful not to be noticed, nevertheless examined these unusual beings with intense curiosity.

Aknus had warned her of the dangers she might face if she were discovered.

Although the visitors appeared to have come for business, the future of Antraka—and of all the surrounding lands—was far too uncertain to risk repeating past experiences.

If there was one thing Hanna could never forget, it was her arrival in this world and the terrifying fate of those she had fortunately managed to leave behind.

Gradually, she adapted to the Snorks' unusual culture.

And once she had gained a stronger command of their language, everything changed dramatically.

Ynack sometimes entertained her with very short stories that Hanna loved to hear.

She became increasingly captivated by the world around her.

Something deep inside urged her to discover more, always more.

Not even her dreams escaped that fascination.

On most nights, Hanna found herself immersed in the battles, adventures, and other tales that Ynack recounted, however briefly.

One morning, while Ynack was preparing lunch as usual, Hanna was exploring on her own.

Behind several large, heavy stones, whose surfaces bore inscriptions worn by years of exposure to the weather, she discovered an enormous scroll.

She had developed a habit of taking advantage of every moment her kindly teacher was absent to search beyond what could be seen at first glance.

Determined to find answers, she never missed an opportunity to investigate anything that might escape Ynack's watchful eyes.

Most of her discoveries amounted to little more than incomprehensible fragments, offering nothing beyond the excitement of finding them.

But that morning, her curiosity was finally rewarded with a genuine discovery.

The scroll appeared ancient.

It was remarkably well preserved, and when she unrolled it, she found herself looking at a large and unfamiliar map.

Hanna studied it intently.

She examined the names of the places depicted there and tried to read them.

Slowly, for reading was still a skill she was teaching herself, she attempted to locate territories she had heard mentioned in Ynack's stories.

Then something caught her attention.

In one particular part of the map, a small area was marked with golden letters.

She could not fully understand what was written there.

But she did not need to.

As her fingers traced the name, letter by letter, a tingling sensation spread throughout her body.

The hairs on her skin stood on end.

Suddenly, she became terribly nervous.

It was her name!

A knot tightened in her stomach.

Overwhelmed with excitement, she seized the map and ran to find Ynack.

Hearing the commotion, the elderly Snork hurried to meet her.

“Hanna! What is happening?” she asked in a raised voice, although her customary composure remained.

“Look, Ynack!” Hanna exclaimed.

She spread the map across a table and immediately pointed to her name on the parchment.

“Where did you find this, little one?” the old Snork asked calmly.

Inside, however, her anxiety was growing with every passing moment.

“What does this mean? This is my name. It's my name!”

The discovery had taken Ynack completely by surprise.

For a moment, she seemed uncertain how to respond.

This information was entirely new to her.

“This is Kettke, little one. The Kingdom of Kingdoms. Kettke! Kettke!”

“Kettke! Kettke!” Hanna repeated, never taking her eyes off Ynack.

It was not the first time she had heard that name.

But hearing it again was no answer.

What she had just discovered made no sense.

She had recognized her own name on a map of a world she had never known before arriving there.

And now Ynack was telling her that the name belonged to a kingdom.

Hanna could not understand it.

Without hesitating, she crossed the room toward one of the walls.

It was made of smooth, hard stone and served as a writing board whenever necessary.

Picking up something resembling a large piece of chalk, she wrote two words in enormous letters, one beneath the other.

“I'll show you!” she said, looking at Ynack, who was watching her every movement.

“Look. This is my name in the land where I grew up. This is how it's written there, and this is what they call me. Hanna!”

She pointed to the first word.

Then she lowered her hand to the second.

“And this is Kettke! But… why? Why are these two words the same?”

Her voice trembled with agitation.

She struck the wall with her hand, struggling to hold back her tears.

Ynack was still shaken, and seeing her pupil in such distress did nothing to calm her.

After drawing a slow breath and letting out a faint sigh, she prepared to answer.

It would not be easy.

This was the first time she had ever seen Hanna's name written in the language of her own world.

They had tried to conceal everything related to Kettke from her.

They had deliberately avoided teaching her its written language.

They had never asked where she came from or how she had arrived.

They were not supposed to learn anything about her, and she was only supposed to know what was necessary.

That was how Vendemur had decreed it.

For now, their task was simply to prepare her for her journey.

That would be enough.

The answers would come, but Hanna had to be ready.

Whatever remained hidden inside her would soon have to emerge.

Before that happened, however, she needed to be safe.

Far from Antraka.

Somewhere evil could not reach her.

Above all, her spirit had to be prepared.

Otherwise, none of it would make sense.

And yet, here and now, Hanna was right.

The two names were identical.

Strangely identical.

But how could that be possible?

Ynack knew more about her perceptive young pupil than anyone else in that place.

Even so, she had no explanation.

The mystery was incomprehensible even to the wise Snork.

Hanna had come from another world, knowing nothing of this one.

Why, then, was her name the same?

The seconds seemed endless.

Hanna watched her teacher with bright, tear-filled eyes, desperately waiting for some revelation.

“A difficult question that is,” Ynack finally began, her voice faltering in a way that was most unusual for her. “I have no answer. Surely there are things that could explain it. But calm you must remain.”

She approached Hanna and gently took her hand.

“These lands hide many secrets, and many of them you must discover. But for now, your questions you must keep. Difficult it is, I know. Yet trust you must, just as we trust in you. Your return home draws near, and hard will be the road that takes you back. For though today you may believe yourself very far from home, in time you may discover that destiny will inevitably lead you there.”

Ynack's words did nothing to reassure Hanna.

Instead, they made her even more uneasy.

Nervous and somewhat upset, she ignored the approaching lunch, whose aroma was already filling the room, and ran outside.

She spent the rest of the day wandering aimlessly.

What could she do?

She faced a terrible dilemma.

Despite the great affection she felt for these creatures, she knew this was not her land.

Not her world.

What had Ynack meant?

A multitude of emotions swept through her, leaving her utterly confused.

Images of Joseph, Kron, and Miss Hings mingled with those of Gunuruk, Ynack, and Aknus.

An awful realization spread through her.

She was completely lost.

So lost that strange, familiar sensations returned, bringing back memories of her arrival in this place.

Nothing could comfort her that day.

Not even the company of the creature who had gradually become her inseparable friend.

Gunuruk knew what had happened.

When he found her, he did everything he could to cheer her up.

But Hanna seemed unwilling to listen to anything he said, although deep down she could not help paying some attention.

“Worry you must not, Hanna,” he repeated tirelessly. “Things I know as well. In us you must trust, for together we must remain. Soon we shall leave, and a long journey awaits us. But always remember that everything that happens may have a purpose, perhaps one already written.”

That afternoon, the little Snork struggled to suppress an overwhelming urge to tell Hanna what he had been guarding so carefully.

But he did not.

He could not.

It was dangerous.

Revealing the secret might change the course of events.

Besides, Gunuruk had worries of his own.

He knew that not everything was unfolding as his grandfather had told him it would.

The winds that season were no different from those of any other year.

Everything else, however, seemed strange.

Small details marked a difference, one that was becoming increasingly noticeable in the atmosphere of the village.

Never before had so many foreigners come to Bettnuk.

And many of them should never have been allowed to stay.

Antraka's laws strictly prohibited doing business with people engaged in evil pursuits.

Of course, Gunuruk could not say for certain that anything unlawful was taking place.

Still less could he accuse Aknus of violating the very laws he had always upheld so proudly.

He continued to follow his grandfather's wishes faithfully.

But he also knew that he had been away from Antraka for a long time.

So long, in fact, that he sometimes felt like a stranger in the place he called home.

Something was clearly taking shape.

Something that would not end well.

And time would eventually prove him right.

As the days passed, life gradually returned to normal.

But Hanna was never quite the same again.

She did everything she could to avoid worrying those who treated her with such kindness.

Nevertheless, her former enthusiasm gave way to apathy.

Constant memories and thoughts of an uncertain future weighed heavily on her usually cheerful face.

After so many days and nights of suffering, she struggled to recover her old spirit.

She no longer searched in secret.

Sometimes she barely paid attention to what anyone said to her.

Only with the passage of time did she manage to focus on one small hope.

Perhaps the beginning of the long journey Ynack, Gunuruk, and Aknus had spoken of so insistently would finally reveal a little light in the seemingly endless darkness that had surrounded her for so long.
